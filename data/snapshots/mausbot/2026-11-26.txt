 What your team gets
 One shared bot, not copies Your team&#x27;s bots live once, on the team&#x27;s Cloud. Set up a bot&#x27;s instructions, skills, apps and MCP servers once, and everyone you share it with uses that same bot. A change reaches them all at once. 
 Private chats stay private Each person&#x27;s own chats with a bot are theirs: no teammate and no admin can read them. Work together in a bot&#x27;s shared chat or a group chat, and Save to team memory keeps a message for everyone. 
 Admins set it up once Admins connect apps and MCP servers for the whole team, or have each person connect their own, and see how each connection is doing on the team&#x27;s admin pages. Team rules set which AI engines and computers bots may use. 
 Keys saved once An admin saves a key once in the team&#x27;s Vault, or connects 1Password for the team, with a dedicated vault and a read-only service account. A bot gets only the secrets it may use, when it needs them, never as text in the chat. 
 Team files One shared folder for the team and its bots, 5 GB. Every bot reads it; admins choose which bots may write, so a payroll bot can keep records from anyone&#x27;s private chat. Each file says who changed it last: "Changed by Mira with Payroll". 
 Team templates Admins save a bot or a whole department to the team&#x27;s templates and add it again from Import. Publish to your company&#x27;s private GitHub, or import from it, through the team&#x27;s GitHub connection. Skills keep their scripts. 
 Your bots in Slack Mention @MausBot in a channel, or message it directly. A direct message is your private chat with the bot. 
 Seats as you grow $40 a month for 2 people, +$20 for each extra person, up to 50. The team&#x27;s owner adds seats from the admin pages; people are invited by email and join in the browser.

 Questions before you pay
 Is AI usage included? +
 No. AI model usage isn&#x27;t included in any plan: you connect your own Claude or ChatGPT account, or an API key, and your provider bills you for it.
 Can I talk to my bots in Slack? +
 Yes, on Team. A team admin adds MausBot to your company&#x27;s Slack from the Apps page of the team&#x27;s admin pages. Then people mention @MausBot in a channel, or message it directly. Each channel has a default bot, and +BotName picks another. A conversation in a channel uses only the team&#x27;s shared memory and apps; a direct message is your private chat with the bot. On Pro and Max, you talk to your own bots in a direct message with MausBot in Slack. Direct messages only, no channels. Not on Starter.
 Can I talk to my bots on a call? +
 Yes, on every plan, the free app included. Live voice calls use your own OpenAI key: add it in Settings → API keys, and OpenAI bills each call to it. The voice included with a Cloud plan is for your bots&#x27; spoken replies.
 What is a computer-hour? +
 One hour of a cloud computer your bots use, with a live desktop you can watch or take over. A small computer counts as half an hour. Allowances reset on the 1st of each month (UTC).
 Do I need to keep my laptop on? +
 Not on a paid plan. Your bots run on your own cloud machine, which stays on around the clock on every Cloud plan, and the phone apps reach them while your laptop is closed. On Free, bots work while your computer is on.
 I could run the free app on my own server. Why pay? +
 You can: the app is open source and always will be. A Cloud plan is for when you would rather not run a server yourself. It comes with cloud computers your bots can drive, voice, automations that run while you sleep, phone access, and updates we look after.
 What happens on the setup call? +
 Pro and Max include 30 minutes with us after you subscribe. We pick the first jobs with you and get those bots running, so you leave the call with work already happening.
 Can I cancel any time? +
 Yes. Plans are billed monthly with no contract, and you can cancel any time from the billing page.
 How do Team seats work? +
 Team is $40 a month for 2 people, the buyer included, and $20 a month for each extra person, up to 50. For example, 5 people pay $100 a month. Each seat is for one person. The team&#x27;s owner, whose card pays, adds and removes seats from the team&#x27;s admin pages, and admins invite people by email into the seats the team has. More than 50 people? Talk to us.
 What happens when I add someone to my team? +
 The team&#x27;s owner adds a seat from the team&#x27;s admin pages, and an admin invites the person by email; they join in the browser, with nothing to install. The plan goes up by $20 a month, and the team&#x27;s shared computer-hours by 100 a month. From 6 people, the team&#x27;s Cloud moves to the larger machine: 8 vCPUs and 8 GB of memory.
 What if someone leaves the team? +
 An admin removes them from the team, which frees their seat for someone else. To stop paying for it, the team&#x27;s owner removes the seat from the team&#x27;s admin pages, and it comes off at the next bill: until then, the plan stays as it is.
 Is it one shared bot or a copy per person? +
 One shared bot. A team&#x27;s bots live once, on the team&#x27;s Cloud: a bot&#x27;s instructions, skills and their scripts, apps and MCP servers are set up once, and everyone it is shared with works with that same bot, so a change reaches them all at once. Each person&#x27;s own chats with it stay private: no teammate and no admin can read them. To work together there is the bot&#x27;s shared chat and group chats, and Save to team memory adds a message to what the bot remembers for the whole team.
 Where do shared files live? +
 In Team files, one shared folder for the team and its bots: 5 GB, up to 100 MB a file. Everyone on the team and every bot can read it. Admins choose which bots may write to it, so a payroll bot can keep its records there from anyone&#x27;s private chat; the chat itself never goes there. Each file shows who changed it last, such as "Changed by Mira with Payroll".
 Can admins set up apps, MCP servers and passwords once for everyone? +
 Yes. An admin connects an app or an MCP server once for the whole team, or has each person connect their own, and sees how each connection is doing on the team&#x27;s admin pages. Team rules set which AI engines and computers the bots may use. Keys and tokens an admin saves in the team&#x27;s Vault are the team&#x27;s, for its bots to use; a key someone types in their own private chat stays theirs. An admin can also connect 1Password for the whole team, with a dedicated vault and a read-only service account. A bot asks for a secret by name and gets it when it needs it, never as text in the chat, and only the bots an item is kept to can use it.
 Can we keep our own templates? +
 Yes. The team&#x27;s owner and admins save a bot or a whole department to the team&#x27;s templates, and add it again from Import, with who added it and when. They can also publish a template to your company&#x27;s private GitHub repository, or import one from it, through the team&#x27;s GitHub connection. A skill&#x27;s scripts travel with it. A template never carries chats, memory, keys or passwords.
