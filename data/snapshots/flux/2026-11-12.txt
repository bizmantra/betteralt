 FAQs
 FLUX 3 Image
 How is pricing calculated?
 Pricing is per image generated. Charges are based on the resolution tier selected in the request, regardless of the resolution at which the image is processed internally, and apply equally to text-to-image and image-to-image generations.
 Reference images, whether provided by the user or retrieved by the model, and prompts are included at no extra charge. The per-image rate does not vary with the number of reference images provided, up to the maximum set out in the documentation.
 The resolution tier is selected in the request; all supported aspect ratios within a tier are charged the same. Delivered dimensions may differ slightly from the nominal size (dimensions are rounded to multiples of 16). Supported resolutions, aspect ratios and reference image limits are set out in the documentation.
 Reference images up to 16 MP are accepted (larger ones are rejected); images above 4 MP are downscaled to 4 MP before processing. Rejected requests, failed generations and outputs withheld by moderation are not charged.
 Tiers denote the approximate pixel count of the output: 768²≈0.6 MP, 1K≈1 MP (1024×1024-equivalent), 1.5K≈2 MP, 2K≈4 MP, 4K≈16 MP.

 FLUX 3 Video
 What counts as Text/Image-to-Video or Video-to-Video?
 Text/Image-to-Video: any generation conditioned solely on text and/or one or more still images, including reference images and keyframes.
 Video-to-Video: any generation conditioned in whole or in part on video input, including continuing, extending, editing or restyling video.
 How are video seconds billed?
 Prices are per second of video output (“Video Second”). Partial seconds are rounded up to the next whole second of the output.
 Rates per Video Second are determined by (a) the generation modality, (b) the model variant selected (e.g., FLUX 3 Video, FLUX 3 Video Draft), and (c) the resolution band of the Output.
 How are resolution bands defined?
 Resolution bands are determined by the total number of pixels per frame in an Output, where one megapixel (MP) equals 1,048,576 pixels (1024×1024).
HD: above 0.5 and up to 1.0 MP per frame (e.g. 1280×704, 960×960, 1440×608).
Full HD: above 1.0 and up to 2.0 MP per frame (e.g. 1920×1088, 1440×1440, 2176×928).
QHD (2K): above 2.0 and up to 4.0 MP per frame (e.g. 2560×1440).
UHD (4K): above 4.0 and up to 8.0 MP per frame (e.g. 3840×2176, 2880×2880).
 Each Output falls within exactly one resolution band regardless of aspect ratio.
 How is Draft mode priced?
 FLUX 3 Video Draft is only available in HD
 FLUX 3 Video Draft Enhance, a full-quality render of a prior draft generation, is priced as a regular generation for the specific mode and the resolution band selected
 Is audio included, and how long can clips be?
 Audio is included at no extra charge.
 Clips up to 20 seconds.

 FLUX Video Tools
 Notes on specific models
 1 .
 One megapixel-second is one megapixel (1,048,576 pixels, 1024 × 1024) of output resolution for one second of output video. Charges are calculated on the delivered output’s actual frame dimensions and duration: megapixels per output frame × output duration in seconds, with fractional values charged pro rata. Example: upscaling a 10-second clip to 1080p (1920 × 1080 = 1.98 megapixels) is 19.8 megapixel-seconds, i.e. $0.69 (Precise) or $0.99 (Creative).
 2 .
 Charges are based on output only. Input resolution, upscale factor and optional prompts are not charged separately.
 3 .
 Rates apply to output at the standard frame rate of 24 frames per second. Where output at a higher frame rate is requested and supported, charges scale proportionally with frame rate (equivalently, the listed rate ÷ 24 per megapixel per frame). Supported input durations, resolutions, upscale factors and frame rates are set out in the documentation.
 4 .
 Input videos up to 20 seconds. Sources longer than 20 seconds are rejected before processing; they are not truncated, and no charge applies.
 5 .
 Pricing is per second of output video. Output duration, frame rate and aspect ratio follow the source video, with fractional seconds charged pro rata.
 6 .
 Charges are based on output only. Input resolution, prompt length and audio passthrough are not charged separately.
 FAQs
 FLUX.2
 How is resolution measured?
 Pricing is per megapixel (MP) of image processed: input pricing per MP for each reference image provided for image editing operations, plus output pricing per MP of output resolution. One megapixel equals 1024 × 1024 pixels (1,048,576 pixels).
 1 MP = 1024×1024 px.
 Resolution is rounded up to the next MP, separately for each reference image and the generated output.
 Resolution measurements are rounded up to the next whole MP, separately for input and output.
 How are reference images charged?
 Multiple reference images each count as 1 MP.
 Reference images: a single reference image up to 4 MP is processed at its original resolution; above 4 MP it is downsampled to and charged as 4 MP. When multiple reference images are provided, each image exceeding 1 MP is downscaled to 1 MP, and each reference image is charged as 1 MP regardless of the output resolution requested.
 What is the maximum output resolution?
 Maximum output resolution is four (4) MP for all operations.
 Images exceeding 4 MP are resized to 4 MP.
