 Teams and Meet have captions?
 Only inside their own apps.
 X Spaces, YouTube Live, Discord and podcasts don’t. Cicada listens to everything your computer plays, or to an in-person meeting room through your microphone: no bot joins the call, and nobody else installs anything.
 Where it works

 Cicada Any app, any web page, any stream, and in-person meeting rooms

 01 · Live subtitles Understand
 What others say turns into subtitles in your language as they speak. It works out which language they’re speaking. On the Mac, speech recognition runs entirely on your computer. On the Mac, Windows and Linux, Cicada can also listen to the meeting room, or to the room and the call together (macOS 26 on the Mac), marking each line In room or Online.
 02 · Interpreting Speak up
 Hold right ⌥ (right Ctrl on Windows) and talk. When you let go, it’s translated into their language and spoken through a virtual microphone. Works in any app you can speak in.
 See how it speaks for you ↓
 03 · Meeting notes Remember
 Notes written while the meeting runs: key points, decisions and to-dos, with speakers separated by voice. Afterwards you keep a bilingual record, or import a recording or video to generate notes, exportable to Markdown, Word and SRT. Misheard names and terms are fixed as you go, and the spellings you use live in a word list for next time.
 See what the meeting leaves behind ↓
 Subtitles work everywhere; interpreting needs a place where you can speak, like a call or a Space. On the Mac, AI notes use a key from an AI service—Ling, OpenRouter, ZenMux, OpenAI or any compatible service; the Windows, Linux and Android apps also hand listening to that service, so they need one. Your own models work too: translation, voice and recognition can each use a different machine, and LAN addresses need no key. Ling gives ¥10 of free credit every month. Get started in three steps →

 Privacy and permissions
 What each permission is for →
 On your Mac On the Mac, audio becomes text on your computer
 Recordings and meeting records stay on your own device. On the Mac, the only things sent out are the recognized text (for translation and notes) and, when you use interpreting, the few sentences you speak while holding the key; if you hand speech recognition to your own server, the meeting audio goes to that machine. The Windows, Linux and Android apps send what is said in short pieces to the AI service you chose.
 Permissions Asked only when needed, optional where possible
 On the Mac, system audio is for subtitles; the microphone records only while you hold the talk key or when “Listen to › Room” is selected; Input Monitoring only watches the right ⌥ (not needed with a key combination). On Mac, Windows and Android the notes window stays hidden when you share your screen.
 Open No tracking, open source
 It only connects to the translation service, the AI service you configure, the official download of the virtual microphone and GitHub. No analytics and no servers of our own. Installers can be checked against their SHA-256.

 Ask a question →
 Will it run on my Mac? You need macOS 14.4 or later. Intel Macs have no on-device speech model, so Ling&#x27;s Ming hears the meeting and a Ling key is required; the rest is about Apple silicon. macOS 26 uses the newer on-device speech recognizer, the most accurate; 14.4 to 15 use the system&#x27;s earlier one, a little weaker, which needs Dictation turned on in System Settings › Keyboard, with each language you want heard added to it.
 What about Windows, Linux and phones? Windows 10 (2004) or later, the Ubuntu 22.04 / 24.04 desktop and Android 10 or later are all supported, with the same meeting records as the Mac. These apps also hand listening to the AI service, so they need Ling, OpenRouter, ZenMux or your own server; Windows and Linux can listen to the room too. On Linux the notes window shows up in screen sharing; on Android, interpreting is face to face through the speaker. The iPhone app is on its way.
 Does it work with Teams, DingTalk, or Google Meet? If your computer can play the audio, Cicada supports it. That includes Tencent Meeting, Lark, DingTalk, WeCom, Zoom, Teams, Google Meet, Webex, Discord, and Telegram—plus browser-based meetings, livestreams, and podcasts. For live interpretation, set the microphone in your meeting app or browser to the virtual microphone (BlackHole 2ch on Mac, VB-Cable on Windows, or Cicada Microphone on Linux). On Android, the system blocks recording audio from meeting and call apps, so you need to put the call on speaker and select In person in Cicada. Regular videos and livestreams on your phone work directly.
 Does it cost anything? Cicada is free and open source. On the Mac, live subtitles, translation and meeting records work without a key; AI notes and natural voices use your own Ling key, about ¥0.15–1 per hour of meeting. The Windows, Linux and Android apps need an AI service. Ling gives ¥10 of credit every month.
 Will the other side know? No bot joins the call, and nobody else installs anything. When you share your screen on Mac, Windows or Android, the notes window stays out of the picture by default.
 Is meeting audio uploaded? On the Mac, audio turns into text on your computer; only the recognized text leaves it, plus the few sentences you say while holding the talk key for interpreting. If you set Settings › AI Service › Speech recognition to Your service, the meeting audio goes to that machine. The Windows, Linux and Android apps send what is said in short pieces to the AI service you chose. Recordings stay on your own device.
 Which languages does it support? On the Mac, any language its on-device speech recognition supports is detected; on the other platforms the AI service you chose recognizes it. Translations go into your system language by default. Natural interpreting voices cover English, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, German, French and Spanish.
 Is what I say out loud recorded? For now, only what you say while holding the talk key. “Record what I say” is first on the roadmap.
 Blocked on first launch? Cicada isn’t notarized by Apple or code-signed for Windows yet. On a Mac, go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and click “Open Anyway”; on Windows, click “More info › Run anyway”. Only once. Proper signing is on the roadmap.
 What else does interpreting need? On the Mac, a virtual microphone, BlackHole: the first time you turn on interpreting, Cicada downloads it from the official source and installs it. On Windows, the free VB-Cable, which the guide helps you install. On Linux nothing: Cicada makes its own “Cicada Microphone”.
